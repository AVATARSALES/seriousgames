Scenario summary
You are recruiters for SPEED-UP, a hyper-growth B2B start-up (Series A raised, “organised chaos”). The sales team must grow from 2 to 10 people in 3 months. The CEO changes strategy every week, there is no structured CRM, and the team has to “bring in revenue” immediately.

Mission: review 4 candidate files for the Senior Business Developer (pure hunting) position, listen to their interviews and choose the profile best suited to this culture.
What the interviews reveal
2 → 10 sales reps to hire in 3 months · 0 structured CRM, product sheet or marketing leads · 1 / week CEO strategy change (on Tuesdays)

→ Empty database and marketing not sending enough leads
→ Buggy product, no brochure, tech team running late
→ Two juniors start on Monday and need to ramp up
→ A competitor launches its offer the day after tomorrow; priority: “bring in revenue” right now

Activity
In teams of 3-4, learners read the 4 application files (CV + simulated interview dialogues), analyse each candidate's behaviours and soft skills in concrete situations (chaos, product bug, team conflict), then deliberate and defend their choice in front of the other teams. The trainer leads a comparative debrief on the soft skills expected in a hyper-growth start-up.
Learning objectives
Knowledge (Know)
Define the key soft skills of a Business Developer in a start-up context (agility, resilience, autonomy, hunter mindset). Distinguish the 4 behavioural archetypes (architect, pirate, mediator, hunter) and their strengths/risks in a sales context.
DefineDistinguish
Skills (Apply)
Analyse a candidate profile based on real behavioural dialogues. Assess the fit between a profile and a specific company culture. Argue a hiring decision with examples taken from the file. Identify the warning signs of an unsuitable candidate (rigidity, legal risk, avoiding targets).
AnalyseAssessArgueIdentify
Attitudes (Adopt)
Adopt an objective assessment stance based on behaviours, not degrees. Demonstrate the ability to defend a point of view against opposing arguments. Develop critical thinking towards profiles that look appealing on paper but are unsuitable in practice.
AdoptDemonstrateDevelop
Teaching format
Format: Collaborative team game — file reading + deliberation + oral presentation

Grouping: Teams of 3 to 5 learners

Total duration: 1 h 30 (module, Excel grid, deliberation, pitch and debrief: see the Method tab)

Assessment: Quality of argumentation + identification of behavioural signals + consistency with the start-up culture

Prerequisites: Basic knowledge of recruitment and sales management

They reflect SPEED-UP's constraints: starting from scratch (autonomy), signing fast (intensity), following a moving strategy (chaos), without exposing the company (risk) and while growing the team fivefold (team culture).
Scoring scale
5 decisive asset · 4 strength · 3 neutral · 2 warning sign · 1 unsuitable
Two answers can be defended: Depending on the company's priority (speed or safety), two candidates can come out on top. Your grid and your arguments must state clearly which priority you choose and why it fits SPEED-UP today.
Three traps to avoid
The degree biasThey all come from the same Top 5 school. The prestige of the CV predicts nothing here.
The safety biasA reassuring profile is not necessarily the one the emergency calls for.
The likeability biasA pleasant candidate is not necessarily suited to the speed required.
The right question is not “Who is the best salesperson?” but “Who will succeed at SPEED-UP, in this specific context, starting tomorrow?”
Your deliverables
→ The team Excel grid (20 justified scores, final choice, 3 arguments)
→ A 3-minute pitch: your choice and why not the other 3
→ Employer branding bonus: a catchphrase for the job ad

A good argument: a fact (who says what), what it reveals, and the SPEED-UP constraint it relates to.

SPEED-UP still has to hire 8 sales reps. The chosen candidate will be the face of the team for the next hires.
